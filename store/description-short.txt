Record what you do on a page. Get a Robot Framework suite that runs.

Most recorders hand you Sleep 2 and nth-child selectors, and you rewrite them
before they can be committed. Zelector works on the two things that decide
whether a recorded test survives.

WAITS — inferred from what the page actually did after each step. A spinner that
comes and goes becomes Wait Until Element Is Not Visible on it. A route change
becomes Wait Until Location Contains. Timeouts come from the time the page took.
Every guess shows its reason and can be edited before you export.

LOCATORS — every candidate scored, every point lost explained. Classes generated
by a build tool, ids regenerated on every render, classes that name a state
rather than an element: marked, not handed over as though they were stable. When
nothing durable exists, the panel says so while you can still ask for a
data-testid.

Closed shadow roots, iframes, alerts, drag and drop, hover menus and rich text
editors are handled — each one a recording that otherwise replays as nothing.

One permission: storage. No servers, no account, no analytics, no network
requests of any kind. MIT, and the source is public.
